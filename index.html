01  Who they actually are

Paul McLaughlin is not a small grower. External reporting describes him as once one of the biggest watermelon producers in Australia.

The operation

Desert Springs Farm, Ali Curung NT

Roughly 400 km north of Alice Springs — the first commercial horticulture operation established on Aboriginal land in the Northern Territory.

Scale

~8,000 t watermelon a year

Plus Central Australia's first commercial peanut harvest — which matches the peanut program sitting in our own files from 2020.

Structure

Leased, Centrefarm-managed

Warrabri Aboriginal Land Trust; head lease held by Alekarenge Horticulture Pty Ltd. Centrefarm developed 21 bores delivering up to 5,000 ML.

He has been diversifying into rotational crops since at least 2021, and our September 2024 quote for asparagus at Alice Springs is part of that. Company registration is NSW (Dubbo registered office, Condobolin postal) but the ABN's main business location is NT 0872 — the Territory operation is the real centre of gravity.

Sizing this honestly

The program is priced per hectare and we do not hold their planted area. If the external figure of ~8,000 t of watermelon is right, typical yields put them somewhere around 130–200 ha — which would make the full program a six-figure annual line and our $55k the small end of it. Treat that as an order-of-magnitude prompt, not a number to quote at him. The first job on the call is simply to ask how many hectares he is planting this season, across melons and the rotation crops.

06  How to open the call

He has 16 years of history with us and was written a full program two seasons ago. The opening should acknowledge the gap rather than pretend it isn't there.

Ask, in this order

  1. Lead with Judah. He pointed us back to Paul — a warm door, worth naming in the first sentence.
  2. The season and the area. Hectares planted, melons versus rotation crops, and whether the Alice Springs asparagus block went ahead. We quoted it in Sep 2024 and there is no order against it.
  3. What happened after January 2025? Nineteen months of silence following his biggest ever order usually means something specific — a season, a price, a competitor, or a person leaving.
  4. Take the program back to him. He runs Power-Cal and Opti-Cal without the Agri-Vive RF and ground-spread blend the program was built around. Agri-Vive RF at 285 L/ha is the largest volume line he is not buying.
  5. Offer a fresh soil test. The most recent soil work in his folder is a 2023 copy of a 2020 test. A current test is the natural reason to re-write the program — and it is how the 2024 push started.

Two seasons have passed since that program was written. Re-testing is defensible on its own merits, not just as a door-opener.

Thread to chase

Nematodes

A SARDI nematode diagnostic submission form sits in his Programs folder. It is a blank template, not a result — evidence the topic was raised, not that testing happened. If nematode pressure is real in those sandy Territory soils it is a strong opening for the soil primers (Mycro-Tec SP, Mycro-Feast) he has never bought.

Thread to chase

Asparagus at Alice Springs

Quoted 17 Sep 2024 at $2,425/ha as a dry prescription blend, 1,800 kg/ha spread rate. No order followed. Asparagus is a perennial with a multi-year establishment spend — if that block exists, it is an annuity, not a one-off.
