A 16-year customer who went quiet after their biggest order

Clean credit, a $50k limit already approved, and a full nutrient program we wrote for them in 2024 that they only ever part-bought. This is a re-engagement and a share-of-wallet call, not a cold pitch.

Who they actually are

Paul McLaughlin is not a small grower. External reporting describes him as once one of the biggest watermelon producers in Australia.

Desert Springs Farm, Ali Curung NT

Roughly 350 km north of Alice Springs (published figures range 320–400 km). Described by Centrefarm to the ABC as the first commercial horticulture operation created on Indigenous land in the NT — Centrefarm developed it, so treat that as their claim rather than an independent finding.

6,000–10,000 t watermelon a year

A published range, not a current rate: up to 6,000 t expected in 2015 (ABC), 10,000 t/yr reported 2018 (Alice Springs News), ~8,000 t on Centrefarm's page — which has carried that same sentence unchanged since at least 2020. Nothing published since 2018. Plus Central Australia's first commercial peanut harvest, which matches the peanut program in our own files from 2020.

Leased, Centrefarm-managed

Warrabri Aboriginal Land Trust; head lease held by Alekarenge Horticulture Pty Ltd. Centrefarm managed a borefield serving several leases on the Trust — 21 bores, production and monitoring combined, up to 5,000 ML. Desert Springs took one of those leases in 2008; this farm's share is not broken out.

He has been rotating crops through the melon ground since at least 2014 — chia trialled that year and on rotation from early 2015, then the peanut harvest in 2020 — and asparagus is a long-standing ambition, not a recent pivot: he named it as suiting the site back in February 2015. Our September 2024 asparagus quote lands on a ten-year-old idea. The ASIC registered office is Level 1, 48 Church Street, Dubbo NSW (unchanged since 2016), but ASIC and the ABR both put the principal place of business at NT 0872 from incorporation — the Territory operation is the registered centre of gravity, not just the practical one.

Sizing this honestly

The program is priced per hectare, per crop, and we do not hold their planted area — the 28 Aug 2024 program is written on a single hectare at $4,693. Public reporting doesn't settle it: the lease is ~1,200 ha with about 400 ha developed for irrigation (Alice Springs News, 2018), but that footprint also carries peanuts, chia and fruit trials, so 400 ha is not a melon number. Working the other way, 6,000–10,000 t at Australian seedless watermelon yields of 20–40 t/ha (NSW DPI gross margin budget) implies roughly 200–400 ha of melons a year, and they run two crops a season. Every one of those numbers is inference — do not quote any of them at him. The first job on the call is to ask how many hectares he is planting this season, across melons and the rotation crops.

How to open the call

He has 16 years of history with us and was written a full program two seasons ago. The opening should acknowledge the gap rather than pretend it isn't there.

Ask, in this order

  1. Judah opened this door, but he is ours, not theirs. He flagged Paul to Nathan as a former director, and he wrote the first Odoo order in Aug 2024 — so he is a good reason for us to call, not an outside introduction to invoke with Paul. Andrew Woodford owns the account and wrote the 2024 program; he is the name that belongs in the conversation.
  2. The season and the area. Hectares planted, melons versus rotation crops, and whether the Alice Springs asparagus block went ahead. We quoted it in Sep 2024 and there is no order against it.
  3. What happened after January 2025? His biggest ever order, then a month-late payment, then nothing for nineteen months. That pattern usually means something specific — a season, a cash-flow pinch, a price, a competitor, or a person leaving.
  4. Take the program back to him. He runs Power-Cal and Opti-Cal without the ground-spread blend and the Agri-Vive RF the program was built around. The 2,000 kg/ha Dry Prescription Blend is the single largest line he is not buying; Agri-Vive RF is the largest unbought liquid at 285 L/ha for the season, spread over 14 applications of 10–30 L.
  5. Offer a fresh soil test. The most recent soil work in his folder is a 2023 re-print of a November 2019 test. A current test is the natural reason to re-write the program — and it is how the 2024 push started.

Thread — nematodes

A SARDI nematode diagnostic submission form sits in his Programs folder. It is a blank template, not a result — evidence the topic was raised, not that testing happened. If nematode pressure is real in those sandy Territory soils it is a strong opening for the soil primers (Mycro-Tec SP, Mycro-Feast) he has never bought.

Thread — asparagus at Alice Springs

Quoted 17 Sep 2024 at $2,425/ha as a dry prescription blend, 1,800 kg/ha spread rate. No order followed. Asparagus is a perennial with a multi-year establishment spend — if that block exists, it is an annuity, not a one-off.
